Pitch File 06 — Defense Questions (What Judges Will Ask)

Q1: "What if your model just predicts safe always?"
A:
- A lazy model predicting Safe gets 61% accuracy but misses 100% of deadly evacuations.
- We reject raw accuracy and optimize for minority-class Recall, Precision, and F1.
- XGBoost achieved 100% recall (0 missed evacuations out of 197) with 97.04% precision.
- Proves the AI actively learns dangerous pre-failure signals rather than defaulting to Safe.

Q2: "Your data is synthetic — how is this real?"
A:
- Sensor data is synthetic due to zero active mine access during the hackathon.
- However, DEM terrain, Sentinel-1 SAR radar, and ERA5 rainfall are 100% real open data.
- Calibrated against NASA GLC, Landslide4Sense, and Dorren et al. for realistic sensor ranges.
- Governed by Fukuzono (1985) physics equations rather than random statistical noise.

Q3: "You claimed InSAR — that's not what you're using."
A:
- Proactively clarified: We use Sentinel-1 GRD amplitude backscatter, NOT phase-based interferometry.
- We do not claim sub-millimeter satellite deformation measurements.
- Backscatter changes serve as a proven proxy for surface moisture and rock roughness.
- GRD was selected because it is terrain-corrected and analysis-ready without heavy phase pipelines.

Q4: "Why not use real sensor data?"
A:
- Active opencast coal mines restrict live sensor API access during competition hackathons.
- Synthetic data is the transparent, honest engineering choice rather than faking field access.
- The pipeline uses frozen Pydantic contracts identical to industrial IoT sensor streams.
- Live mines can plug physical sensors directly into our API with zero code modifications.

Q5: "How do you handle class imbalance?"
A:
- Applied balanced class weighting via scikit-learn to heavily penalize missed evacuations.
- Explicitly rejected SMOTE because sensor channels are physically coupled by geomechanical laws.
- SMOTE would create impossible readings, like high pore pressure during zero rainfall on dry rock.
- Class weighting preserves authentic physics while driving XGBoost to 100% evacuation recall.

Q6: "Why temporal split instead of random?"
A:
- Random splits cause temporal leakage by mixing future failure states into past training sets.
- Models trained on random splits memorise specific collapse curves rather than learning general physics.
- We split with a hard cutoff date at 2026-06-03 across all 16 zones simultaneously.
- Strictly tests the model's ability to forecast future unseen events in deployment.

Q7: "Is 97.98% accuracy good enough?"
A:
- Accuracy is NOT our primary metric due to the 61/26/13 class imbalance.
- Our primary life-safety metric is Evacuation Recall: 100% for XGBoost and 98.48% for RF.
- XGBoost caught all 197 evacuation events on the unseen test set with 0 misses.
- Proves the system prioritizes worker safety over deceptive overall accuracy numbers.

Q8: "What about false alarms?"
A:
- XGBoost achieved 97.04% precision, meaning only ~3% of evacuation alerts were false alarms.
- In life-safety engineering, a false alarm is a temporary inconvenience, but a miss is a fatality.
- RF provides an even tighter 99.49% precision for mines demanding ultra-low false alarm rates.
- Both models deliver actionable alerts well within standard mining operational tolerances.

Q9: "Why SHAP and what does it prove?"
A:
- SHAP mathematically proves the models learn beyond simple displacement threshold cutoffs.
- In v1, terrain features had 0% importance because danger labels ignored geology.
- In v2, terrain contribution rose to 6.90% in XGBoost and 17.03% in RandomForest.
- Proves satellite radar and DEM slope genuinely shift risk decisions at boundary zones.

Q10: "What's the RF 30.31% terrain artifact?"
A:
- In isolation tests (v2b/v2c), RF terrain importance stayed constant at 30.31%.
- Caused by spatial autocorrelation: steep slope angles naturally correlate with designated high-risk zones.
- RF learned zone identity rather than pure marginal slope dynamics under hard clips.
- We report this transparently, demonstrating honest scientific critique over inflated claims.

Q11: "How does this compare to SSR?"
A:
- SSR is the gold standard: sub-millimeter precision, $250k-$500k cost, line-of-sight only.
- Our system is a complementary extension covering the 20-40% blind spots SSR cannot see.
- Evaluates 10 multi-modal features rather than relying on a single displacement point sensor.
- Brings automated slope safety monitoring to hundreds of mines that cannot afford SSR.

Q12: "What's the alert lead time?"
A:
- Delivers sub-minute alert latency from sensor ingestion to live WebSocket dashboard updates.
- Literature reference (IEEE CNN-LSTM paper) targets ~30-minute mean early warning lead times.
- Catches pre-failure acceleration hours before collapse during the Fukuzono creep phase.
- Edge deployment eliminates internet latency, firing sirens within milliseconds on site.

Q13: "Can this work offline at a remote mine?"
A:
- Runs fully offline on Raspberry Pi or Jetson using lightweight ONNX Runtime inference.
- Directly triggers physical relays, sirens, flashing strobes, and GSM SMS without internet.
- Cloud dashboard synchronizes opportunistically whenever cellular or broadband returns.
- Keeps life-saving emergency warning loops 100% operational during remote network outages.

Q14: "Why not LSTM/GRU first?"
A:
- Followed standard ML engineering hierarchy: establish strong tree baselines (RF/XGBoost) first.
- Tree models provide fast CPU inference, robust tabular handling, and exact SHAP explainability.
- Added PyTorch GRU in Phase 18-19 to evaluate 14-day temporal sequence dynamics.
- Proves architectural scalability from simple tree models to deep recurrent neural networks.

Q15: "What's the cost of inaction?"
A:
- A major slope collapse causes fatalities and financial losses exceeding 500 crore rupees.
- Causes extended DGMS regulatory shutdowns costing 5 to 15 crore rupees per day.
- Destroys high-value draglines and hydraulic shovels worth tens of crores each.
- Deploying a low-cost automated warning system is a minor investment that protects human lives.
