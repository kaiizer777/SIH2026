Pitch File 03 — ML & Data Science

Q1: What is the central ML challenge in your landslide risk classification system?
A:
- Severe class imbalance: 61% Safe, 26% Warning, and only 13% Evacuation.
- A lazy majority-class model gets ~61% accuracy but misses 100% of deadly evacuations.
- We used balanced class-weighted loss to heavily penalize missed evacuation events.
- Both RF and XGBoost achieve >97% accuracy while maintaining near-perfect evacuation recall.

Q2: What happens if your model always predicts safe, and how do you guard against it?
A:
- Always predicting Safe gives a deceptive ~61% accuracy while causing catastrophic safety failures.
- It produces 0% recall on Evacuation, ignoring every dangerous slope approaching failure.
- We guard against this by evaluating minority-class precision, recall, F1, and raw confusion matrices.
- XGBoost caught 100% of evacuations (0 misses), and RF caught 98.48% (only 3 borderline misses).

Q3: Why do you use class-weighted loss instead of SMOTE for handling class imbalance?
A:
- Sensor channels are physically correlated per geomechanical laws (Fukuzono inverse velocity curve).
- SMOTE averages neighboring points, generating physically impossible readings (e.g. high pressure with zero rain).
- Class weighting preserves 100% real observations, penalizing missed evacuations 5.4 times harder.
- Both RF and XGBoost use identical sample weights for an honest, controlled benchmark.

Q4: Why did you use a temporal split instead of random train-test splitting?
A:
- Random splitting causes temporal leakage by leaking future slope trajectories into the training set.
- Landslide deformation is sequential: models that see the future memorise curves instead of learning physics.
- We split with a hard date cutoff at 2026-06-03 across all 16 zones simultaneously.
- 3,648 rows for training/validation and 1,136 future rows strictly held out for testing.

Q5: What are the 10 features in your model's feature vector and what does each represent?
A:
- 3 terrain features: slope angle, aspect direction, and profile curvature from Copernicus DEM.
- 2 satellite radar features: Sentinel-1 VV (surface roughness) and VH (structural volume scattering).
- 1 environmental feature: daily rainfall from Open-Meteo ERA5 reanalysis.
- 4 geotechnical sensor channels: displacement rate, ground vibration, pore water pressure, and microstrain.

Q6: Why do you report both RandomForest and XGBoost, and how do they compare?
A:
- Both models are trained with identical 300 estimators and balanced class weights.
- XGBoost reaches 97.98% accuracy and 100% recall (0 missed evacuations out of 197).
- RF achieves 97.71% accuracy and 0.9898 F1, with fewer false alarms (0.9949 precision).
- Reporting both ensures transparency, proving high performance is robust across algorithm architectures.

Q7: What does XGBoost's 100% evacuation recall actually mean in practice?
A:
- Correctly detected all 197 evacuation events on the unseen test set with zero misses.
- Guarantees that every dangerous slope crossing critical failure thresholds triggered an evacuation alert.
- Achieves 97.04% precision, meaning only ~3% of evacuation alerts were false alarms.
- Provides a proven, life-safety benchmark prioritized for zero undetected slope collapses.

Q8: How does SHAP analysis prove your models learn beyond displacement thresholds?
A:
- SHAP breaks down each prediction into exact mathematical percentage contributions per feature.
- Terrain and SAR radar account for 17.03% of RF importance and 6.90% of XGBoost importance.
- In v1, terrain importance was 0% in XGBoost because labels ignored geology completely.
- Proves our AI genuinely learns mountain terrain context rather than acting as a 1D sensor threshold.

Q9: What was the v1 -> v2 label fix, and why did it matter for model learning?
A:
- v1 assigned danger labels purely on raw displacement, completely ignoring slope steepness and radar.
- v2 introduced a composite score: Risk = Displacement x Terrain Susceptibility Multiplier (0.70 to 1.30).
- Dangerous steep slopes now escalate to Evacuation much earlier than flat, stable benches.
- Increased RF terrain SHAP from 12.27% to 18.63%, creating genuine multi-modal feature learning.

Q10: What do the v2b and v2c isolation tests prove about your range-widening mechanism?
A:
- v2b tested the terrain multiplier alone under v1 hard-clipped displacement ranges.
- v2c tested an aggressive multiplier range [0.50, 1.60], generating only 3 crossover pairs (0.05%).
- Proves the multiplier acts as a margin fine-tuner rather than artificially destabilizing class distributions.
- Validates that the 75 crossover pairs in v2 reflect genuine, physically meaningful risk shifts.

Q11: Why is your synthetic data based on the Fukuzono method rather than random generation?
A:
- Uses the Fukuzono (1985) Inverse Velocity Method where inverse velocity (1/v) trends linearly to zero.
- Sensor channels (displacement, vibration, pore pressure, strain) co-vary according to soil mechanics.
- Calibrated against 4 external benchmarks: Landslide4Sense, NASA GLC, Dorren et al., and DGMS data.
- Ensures synthetic data shares the physical structure and statistical distributions of real slope failures.

Q12: What are the 4 correlated sensor channels and what physical process does each capture?
A:
- Displacement (mm/day): Direct slope movement rate, accelerating exponentially before collapse.
- Vibration (0.01-0.90 g): Microseismic tremors caused by internal rock cracking and stress adjustment.
- Pore Pressure: Subsurface groundwater pressure that reduces friction along potential failure planes.
- Strain (50-1600 με): Internal microstrain rock deformation occurring prior to visible surface sliding.

Q13: Why is accuracy not your headline metric, and what do you report instead?
A:
- Raw accuracy is deceptive under class imbalance because predicting "Safe" always scores 61%.
- We report evacuation-class Precision (0.9949), Recall (0.9848), F1-Score (0.9898), and PR-AUC.
- We show raw missed evacuation counts (3 for RF, 0 for XGBoost) for maximum clarity.
- Focuses directly on life-safety outcomes rather than inflated vanity statistics.

Q14: Why do you show confusion matrices with raw counts rather than just percentages?
A:
- Percentages can hide error scale, whereas raw counts make results completely auditable.
- Showing RF missed 3 out of 197 evacuations proves the exact margin of error.
- Demonstrates the test set contains 197 real minority-class events, not a cherry-picked handful.
- Confirms statistical credibility across the 912-sample validation and 1,136-sample test sets.

Q15: What is your model progression plan and why are RF/XGBoost just the first phase?
A:
- Phase 1: Robust tree ensembles (RandomForest, XGBoost) for fast tabular inference and SHAP explainability.
- Phase 2: Recurrent sequence models (GRU/LSTM) to evaluate 14-day temporal acceleration and rainfall lag.
- Phase 3: Hybrid CNN-LSTM architectures fusing spatial satellite grids with time-series sensor feeds.
- Validates the data pipeline step-by-step, providing transparent development progress to judges.
