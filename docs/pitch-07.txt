PITCH SECTION 07 — Deep Learning Benchmark (GRU vs Tree Baseline)

HEADLINE NUMBERS (evacuation class, test set, 1,136 held-out sequences):
- RandomForest:  Precision 0.9949 | Recall 0.9848 | F1 0.9898 | Missed evacuations: 3/197
- XGBoost:       Precision 0.9704 | Recall 1.0000 | F1 0.9850 | Missed evacuations: 0/197
- GRU:           Precision 1.0000 | Recall 0.7208 | F1 0.8378 | Missed evacuations: 55/197

WHY GRU WAS INCLUDED (say this proactively, don't wait to be asked):
Tree baselines already capture the strong physical signal in our synthetic Fukuzono-curve
data extremely well. GRU was benchmarked to validate the model progression against the
literature-standard approach (RF/XGBoost -> LSTM/GRU -> hybrid ensemble) and to confirm our
architecture scales to a deep-learning approach for when real, noisier sensor data replaces
synthetic data -- a setting where temporal sequence models are expected to earn their keep
more than they do here.

WHY GRU OVER LSTM (if asked):
At ~3,440 training sequences with a 14-day window, GRU's ~25% fewer parameters than LSTM
give it better small-dataset generalization with no accuracy trade-off -- verified against
current benchmarks, not a shortcut. LSTM's extra gating pays off on longer or noisier
sequences than this physics-clean dataset has.

THE KEY DEFENSIBLE TALKING POINT (this is the one to lead with, it reframes a "weaker"
number into a real finding):
GRU trades recall for precision at the class boundary -- it never raises a false evacuation
alarm (perfect 1.0000 precision), and it never silently downgrades a real evacuation to
safe (zero evacuation->safe misses). All 55 misses land one class away, in warning, not two
classes away in safe. That is a materially safer failure mode than a random miss would be,
and it's a property worth characterizing further once real (non-synthetic) sensor noise
enters the picture.

IF PRESSED ON WHY GRU "LOST":
Say it plainly, don't hedge: on this clean synthetic data, trees win, and that's expected --
we said so in our own methodology notes before we even ran the test set. A hackathon judge
respects a team that predicted its own result and reported it honestly over one that hid a
weaker number or inflated it. This is a controlled comparison, not our best-foot-forward
number -- our shipped production endpoint uses RandomForest for exactly this reason (see
Phase 20 model choice).

DO NOT SAY:
- Do not claim GRU "matches" or "is competitive with" the tree baseline on evacuation F1 --
  0.84 vs 0.99 is a real, material gap, don't understate it.
- Do not claim SHAP was run on GRU -- it wasn't (out of scope, tree explainers don't apply
  directly to RNN outputs, a separate DeepExplainer/GradientExplainer pass would be required
  and was deliberately not pursued this cycle).
