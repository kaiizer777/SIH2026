Pitch File — Model Selection & SHAP Interpretability

Q1: What is SHAP and how does it work at a conceptual level?
A:
- SHAP stands for SHapley Additive exPlanations, based on Nobel Prize-winning cooperative game theory.
- Think of every prediction as a team game where all 10 features are team players.
- SHAP calculates the exact percentage credit or blame each feature deserves for the final risk score.
- For every single mine reading, it tells us exactly how much slope, radar, or displacement contributed.

Q2: Why did we run SHAP analysis and what specifically were we trying to prove?
A:
- To prove our AI actually uses satellite radar and terrain data, not just raw displacement sensors.
- A lazy AI model would only check if displacement is high and ignore the mountain geology.
- SHAP proves our model combines all sensors, weather, and satellite maps together for its predictions.
- We verified this on the 912-sample validation set and confirmed identical patterns on the test set.

Q3: What do the terrain/SAR SHAP contribution numbers mean — RF 17.03% vs XGBoost 6.90%?
A:
- Terrain/SAR features include slope angle, curvature, aspect, and Sentinel-1 satellite radar moisture.
- In RandomForest, terrain and satellite radar drive 17.03% of the total decision-making score.
- In XGBoost, terrain and satellite radar only account for 6.90% because it greedily over-focuses on displacement.
- This proves RandomForest genuinely learns mountain physics and geological risk, while XGBoost acts like a simple threshold.

Q4: Why does the v2 labelling fix (risk_score composite) matter for SHAP results?
A:
- In v1, danger labels were based only on raw displacement numbers, so terrain data was ignored.
- In v2, we created a composite score: Risk = Displacement * Terrain Susceptibility Multiplier (0.70 to 1.30).
- This means a steep, wet slope becomes an Evacuation alert much earlier than a flat, dry area.
- This fix made terrain SHAP jump from near 0% in v1 up to 17.03% in v2.

Q5: What are "crossover pairs" and why are they the key to making contextual features matter?
A:
- Crossover pairs are two mine zones with the exact same movement speed (e.g. 48 mm/day)
  but different danger levels.
- A flat bench moving at 48 mm/day is Safe, but a steep 75-degree wet wall
  moving at 48 mm/day triggers Warning.
- In v1, there were 0 crossover pairs because the labels ignored slope angle completely.
- In v2, our physics engine created 75 crossover pairs, teaching the AI how geology changes danger.

Q6: Walk through the RF v2 production model metrics — why is it the production choice?
A:
- RandomForest v2 achieved 97.71% accuracy, 99.49% precision, 98.48% recall, and a 0.9898 F1-score.
- It only missed 3 out of 197 evacuation events on the test set, classifying them safely as Warning.
- In comparison, deep learning GRU missed 55 out of 197 evacuations (F1 of only 0.8378).
- We chose RF because it has the best F1 balance, highest terrain awareness, and runs
  in 1 millisecond on CPU.

Q7: Why not choose XGBoost which has 0 missed evacuations?
A:
- XGBoost caught all 197 evacuations, but had more false alarms (lower precision: 97.04% vs RF's 99.49%).
- In real mining, false alarms cause siren fatigue and shut down operations, costing 5 to
  15 crore rupees per day.
- XGBoost also only had 6.90% terrain SHAP contribution, meaning it behaves like a brittle one-sensor model.
- RandomForest provides the best operational balance: 98.48% recall with virtually zero false alarms.

Q8: Why class weighting over SMOTE?
A:
- Real mine accidents are rare, creating an imbalanced dataset: 61% Safe, 26% Warning, and 13% Evacuation.
- SMOTE creates fake synthetic points by averaging neighbors, which creates physically impossible sensor readings.
- For example, SMOTE might invent a sample with high groundwater pressure during zero rainfall on dry rock.
- Class weighting keeps all data 100% real, but penalizes the AI 5.4 times harder if
  it misses an evacuation.

Q9: How does SHAP feature attribution support the pitch narrative to judges?
A:
- It gives judges mathematical proof that our Copernicus DEM and Sentinel-1 satellite pipelines actually matter.
- We can open a SHAP graph and show exactly why a specific zone triggered an
  alert on a given day.
- It turns our AI from a suspicious black box into an explainable, auditable safety tool for mine managers.
- It proves our multi-modal sensor fusion is a real engineering architecture, not just hackathon buzzwords.

Q10: What is the limitation of SHAP in this context and how would you address it in a follow-up?
A:
- Standard TreeSHAP calculates feature credit on static snapshots, not across 14-day continuous time sequences.
- It also treats correlated features separately instead of grouping related sensor clusters together.
- For next steps, we will implement Grouped-SHAP to score entire sensor packages (geotechnical vs weather vs satellite).
- We will also deploy TimeSHAP to show how displacement accumulated over the past 14 days triggered the alert.
